## 💡 **3. Tips Learned from the Evaluation**

- Adding even **one extra character** can significantly increase password strength.  
- **Randomized combinations** are far more effective than predictable patterns (like `P@ssw0rd`).  
- **Symbols and numbers** placed randomly make brute-force attacks harder.  
- **Long passwords** are much more secure — every extra character multiplies the difficulty.  
- Use **password managers** to safely store complex passwords.

---

## 🛠️ **4. Best Practices for Creating Strong Passwords**

1. Use **12–16 characters minimum**.  
2. Combine **uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and special symbols**.  
3. Avoid **predictable words or sequences** (e.g., names, DOB, “12345”).  
4. **Don’t reuse** passwords across different sites.  
5. Create **unique random combinations** or use **passphrases**.  
6. Enable **Two-Factor or Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**.  
7. **Update passwords** regularly.  
8. **Use password managers** for secure storage.  
9. Avoid sharing passwords or writing them down.  
10. Check strength with **online password checkers**.

---

## 🔍 **5. Research on Common Password Attacks**

- **Brute Force Attack:**  
  Tries every possible combination until a match is found.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Use long, complex passwords and account lockouts.

- **Dictionary Attack:**  
  Uses common words or passwords to guess easily.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Avoid real words; use random mixes.

- **Phishing Attack:**  
  Tricks users into entering passwords on fake websites.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Verify URLs and enable MFA.

- **Keylogger Attack:**  
  Malware records keystrokes to steal passwords.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Keep antivirus active and avoid suspicious downloads.

- **Credential Stuffing:**  
  Reuses stolen password lists on other platforms.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Use **unique passwords** for every account.

- **Social Engineering:**  
  Manipulates users to reveal passwords.  
  🔒 *Prevention:* Never share credentials and verify requests.

---

## 🔐 **6. How Password Complexity Affects Security**

- Password strength increases **exponentially** with **length and randomness**.  
- Examples:  
  - 6-character lowercase → **cracked in seconds**  
  - 12-character mixed → **may take centuries**  
- Complexity and unpredictability make **brute-force and dictionary attacks ineffective**.  
- Using symbols, mixed case, and long phrases ensures **maximum entropy**.
